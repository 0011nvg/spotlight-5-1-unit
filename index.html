Today’s rule: clear English beats fast English. Pilots train specialist aviation language later; first, build strong basics.
Mission briefing

From textbook language to confident use.

Core route: about 60–70 minutes with a tutor. Extra Runway: optional practice for a longer lesson or homework.

By the end, you can: talk about school subjects and objects; use numbers 11–20 and days; introduce yourself; use a/an, pronouns and to be; understand short school texts and audio; write a clear profile; hold a short conversation without reading a script.

Pilot connection

Why does English matter?

In international operations, pilots and air traffic controllers need strong speaking and listening skills in the language used for radiotelephony, including English. That does not mean learning cockpit jargon today.

Today’s foundation is simpler: listen carefully, say numbers clearly, ask precise questions and confirm information.
